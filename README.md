# Sprint-Review-and-Retrospective

**How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?**

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user wants to accomplish and then breaking those needs down into specific features or requirements that can be developed and tested. Instead of only focusing on the technical side of a program, it is important to think about the program from the user's point of view. This helps make sure that the final product actually solves the user's problem.

Creating user stories helps with this because they describe a feature from the perspective of the person who will use it. The format of "As a user, I want..., so that..." makes the purpose of a feature easier to understand. During the SNHU Travel project, user stories helped turn customer requests into smaller pieces of work that the Scrum Team could prioritize, develop, and test. They also made it easier to understand why a feature was needed instead of just focusing on what needed to be programmed.

**How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?**

When developing programs, I like to break larger problems into smaller and more manageable tasks. This makes it easier to focus on one part of the program at a time and test my work as I go. I have learned that development does not always go exactly according to the original plan, so being able to make changes and improve the program throughout development is important.

In my future development work, I would like to use Agile practices such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. I also think maintaining a prioritized Product Backlog is useful because it keeps the team focused on the most important work. Working in shorter Sprints gives developers opportunities to receive feedback and make changes instead of waiting until the entire project is finished. Retrospectives are another practice I would like to continue using because they give the team a chance to look at what worked, what did not work, and what could be improved during the next Sprint.

**What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?**

To me, being a good team member in software development means communicating, being dependable, and being willing to work with others. Software projects usually require people with different roles and skills, so everyone needs to communicate and understand how their work affects the rest of the team. A good team member should be willing to ask questions when something is unclear, help teammates when possible, and communicate problems before they cause larger delays.

This course also showed me that being a good team member means being open to feedback and change. Agile teams work together toward the same goal, and sometimes priorities or requirements will change during development. Being flexible and participating in Scrum events helps the entire team stay informed and continue moving forward. Overall, I think strong teamwork is just as important as technical skills when developing successful software.
